# Canine Transfusion Survival Analysis

## Status

Working comparative analysis for Oxygen Delivery Evidence.

Author: Archil Jaliashvili  
Date: 8 September 2026

## Question

What do published canine transfusion and severe-anemia outcome data suggest about the approximate survival benefit associated with modern transfusion-supported care, and what are the limits of making that comparison?

## Important distinction

The approximate **+20 to 30 percentage-point survival gain** discussed in BHOC Veterinary is a **Derived Analysis** from published outcome ranges.

It is not:
- a single randomized treatment effect
- a universal transfusion effect
- a formal meta-analysis
- proof that transfusion alone caused the observed survival difference

The value should always be labeled as author-derived comparative analysis.

## Published transfusion-supported outcome signals

### Callan et al. 1996
- 307 dogs received RBC transfusion.
- 187 dogs were discharged alive.
- Discharge survival: approximately 61%.

Source: PMID 8784721.

### Hann et al. 2014
- 3,095 dogs received 5,412 pRBC units.
- Hospital discharge survival across visits: 64.8%.
- Thirty-day survival after the first pRBC transfusion: 51.9% overall.
- Thirty-day survival by major anemia category included 52.1% for hemorrhage, 59.1% for hemolysis and 44.7% for ineffective erythropoiesis.

Source: PMID 25311875; PMCID PMC4895626; DOI 10.1111/jvim.12430.

### Radulescu et al. 2021
- 194 dogs received pRBCs in a randomized leukoreduction study.
- 156 survived to discharge.
- Approximate discharge survival: 80.4%.
- Acute transfusion reactions occurred in 16/194 dogs, approximately 8.24%.

Source: PMID 33960540; PMCID PMC8162603; DOI 10.1111/jvim.16138.

### Hall et al. 2024
- Prospective multicenter study of 858 dogs receiving 1,542 allogeneic blood products.
- Acute reaction incidence for pRBC transfusions was reported as 8.9%.

Source: PMID 39239720; PMCID PMC11423481; DOI 10.1111/jvim.17175.

## Severe anemia and IMHA context

Published canine IMHA and severe-anemia cohorts show wide mortality ranges across eras, centers, disease severity and treatment protocols. Modern management frequently includes transfusion, immunosuppression, thromboprophylaxis, intensive monitoring and other supportive measures.

Because these components change together, observed improvements in survival over time cannot be attributed to transfusion alone.

The repository therefore treats the +20 to 30 percentage-point figure as a comparative clinical signal, not a causal transfusion effect.

## Derived Analysis

### Working interpretation

Across published severe-anemia and transfusion-supported outcome ranges, modern treatment-era survival can be materially higher than historical severe-disease survival. The magnitude of the difference commonly falls in the approximate range of **20 to 30 percentage points** in the comparisons used in the BHOC Veterinary analysis.

### Correct label

**BHOC / Oxygen Delivery Evidence Derived Analysis: approximate net survival gain +20 to 30 percentage points across selected published outcome ranges.**

### Required publication note

This value is derived from heterogeneous published populations and should not be interpreted as a pooled treatment effect or as the expected benefit of transfusion in an individual patient.

## Why the analysis is still clinically useful

The point is not to diminish transfusion. Donor blood is lifesaving and remains standard therapy when oxygen-carrying capacity is inadequate.

The analysis highlights three separate realities:

1. Survival improves with modern critical care and transfusion-supported management, but substantial mortality remains.
2. Donor RBC therapy adds biological complexity, including compatibility, storage, transfusion reactions and alloimmunization.
3. The final physiological objective remains restoration of effective oxygen delivery to tissues.

## Comparator evidence that should be tracked with survival

Future updates should extract study-level values for:
- disease category
- baseline severity
- pre-transfusion PCV / Hct / Hb
- indication for transfusion
- whole blood vs pRBC
- transfusion volume
- number of transfusions
- timing of transfusion
- lactate
- shock status
- survival to discharge
- 7-day survival
- 30-day survival
- transfusion reactions
- euthanasia vs spontaneous death
- confounders such as financial euthanasia

## What should not be claimed

Do not write:
- "donor blood gives only 20 to 30% survival"
- "transfusion increases survival by exactly 20 to 30% in all dogs"
- "Oxyglobin has 95% survival"
- "95% Oxyglobin efficacy can be directly compared with 51.9% 30-day pRBC survival"

These statements mix different populations and endpoints.

## Research question generated by the analysis

If conventional transfusion-supported care can materially improve survival but remains dependent on donor availability, compatibility, storage, logistics and time, can an immediately available cell-free oxygen carrier create an additional layer of stabilization before or alongside donor transfusion?

That question should be tested using oxygen-delivery endpoints as well as survival.
