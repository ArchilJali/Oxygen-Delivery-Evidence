# Evidence Methodology

## Core principle

Published evidence, derived analysis and scientific interpretation must remain visibly distinct.

### Published Evidence
A result, endpoint, observation or conclusion reported directly by an external scientific, regulatory or professional source.

### Derived Analysis
A calculation or structured comparison performed from published source values. Derived analyses must preserve the source values, calculation method, populations, endpoint definitions and limitations.

### Scientific Perspective
An interpretation of the combined evidence and its implications for research, development or clinical strategy.

### Research Hypothesis
A testable question arising from the evidence that has not yet been established.

## Source hierarchy

1. Primary regulatory records
2. Peer-reviewed primary research
3. Consensus statements and clinical guidelines
4. Systematic reviews
5. Peer-reviewed narrative reviews
6. Professional reviews
7. Institutional and public-authority context
8. Secondary commentary

## Copyright handling

The database stores citations, identifiers, factual metadata, structured summaries and source links. Full copyrighted journal text or PDFs should not be copied into the repository unless the license or rights holder clearly permits that use.

## Duplicate control

A publication is stored once as one master record. The same record can appear in multiple topic, domain and evidence-role views through metadata tags.
